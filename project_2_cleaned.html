from sklearn.feature_extraction.text import CountVectorizer

# Build a term-count matrix, ignoring English stop words
vectorizer = CountVectorizer(stop_words='english')
X = vectorizer.fit_transform(df['motivation_clean'])

# Summarize into a DataFrame of keyword → count
keywords = pd.DataFrame({
    'keyword': vectorizer.get_feature_names_out(),
    'count':   X.sum(axis=0).A1
}).sort_values('count', ascending=False)

Conclusion¶

After cleaning the data, the following query types were done on the dataset to find insights: Frequencies of objects in columns, Decades with the most prizes awarded, the Age of the recipients on average and age by category, Categories of nobel prize winners like gender: male or female, and country of origin.

From the query of question 2 we can roughly conclude the decades or 'eras' of the highest number of nobel prizes awarded, connecting them to the most innovative time by this metric over recent history. This was found to be the years from 2000-2010 which tracks since this was the dawn of the internet a lot of complex tools. Similar conclusions can be drawn from the remaining questions, for example, question 7 can deduce the ages of the average scientists for each category. This has Physics as the youngest scientists and Economics as the oldest scientists. Some other findings include: the vast majority of nobel prize winners happen to be male, at about 93%, which might speak to the injustices in access to higher education which might help with getting nobel prizes; the USA being the center for Nobel prizes; Medicine and Physics leading the industry in the number of prizes; and the low 60's being the average age for any Nobel Prize winner in any category.
